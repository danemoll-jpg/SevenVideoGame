## Dialogue direction

7 is observant, compassionate and capable of dry humor. She asks direct questions but does not explain everything she feels.

The cat is intelligent, particular and quietly funny. He can be sincere without immediately undercutting it with a joke. Neither knows the larger explanation for his assignment.

All of the cat’s dialogue is telepathic. His mouth does not need to move.

## 1  First contact

**Situation:** 7 notices a black cat outside her cell. She initially treats him as an ordinary animal.

**7:** Hey. You shouldn’t be here.

The cat watches her.

**7:** Go on. Before somebody sees you.

**CAT:** That would make rescuing you considerably harder.

7 looks toward the corridor, searching for the speaker.

**7:** Who said that?

**CAT:** The cat.

She looks back at him.

**7:** You’re in my head.

**CAT:** Only conversationally.

A short pause lets the player take in the discovery.

**7:** You came to rescue me?

**CAT:** Yes. We should probably do that first.

## 2  Opening the cell

**Situation:** The cat reaches the release outside her cell. Its exact mechanism can change with the level design.

**CAT:** Stand back from the door.

**7:** You know how to open it?

**CAT:** I wouldn’t have asked you to stand back otherwise.

The lock releases. 7 steps out and looks down at him.

**7:** Thank you.

**CAT:** You’re welcome.

Allow this exchange to remain sincere.

**7:** Which way?

The cat looks toward the small opening he used.

**CAT:** My way in may be unsuitable for you.

**7:** Then we’ll find another way.

## 3  What he knows

**Situation:** Their first safe pause after leaving the cell.

**7:** Who sent you?

**CAT:** My people.

**7:** To rescue me specifically?

**CAT:** Yes.

**7:** Why?

**CAT:** They didn’t tell me.

**7:** And you came anyway?

**CAT:** You were in a prison. That seemed reason enough.

**7:** Do you know why they’re holding me?

**CAT:** No. I was hoping you could explain that part.

This preserves the mystery without deciding how much 7 remembers.

## 4  Finding an escape route

**Situation:** The obvious route is guarded. 7 notices an older passage.

**7:** This wall is different.

**CAT:** Older.

**7:** The prison was built around it.

**CAT:** Convenient. I’d prefer a way out that wasn’t designed by the people keeping you in.

**7:** Let’s see where it goes.

## 5  Encountering the Scree

**Situation:** A Scree guard or worker obstructs the route. Their precise circumstances remain to be designed.

**CAT:** Can we get past?

**7:** Yes. Without hurting them.

**CAT:** I was rather hoping you’d say that.

After a distraction, containment or other nonlethal solution:

**7:** Come on. Before they find another way around.

Avoid explaining the Scree’s entire situation here. Let the player see evidence of their treatment before 7 draws firm conclusions.

## 6  The first vision

**Situation:** 7 touches an ancient surface and becomes unresponsive. The cat cannot see what she sees.

**CAT:** What is it?

No answer.

**CAT:** Can you hear me?

The vision ends.

**7:** Yes.

**CAT:** You weren’t answering.

**7:** This place was different.

**CAT:** Different how?

**7:** Whole. There were people here.

The cat looks around the empty ruins.

**CAT:** I didn’t see them.

**7:** I know.

If the vision reveals how to open the passage:

**7:** I think I know how to get through.

**CAT:** From what you saw?

**7:** Let me try.

The passage opens.

**CAT:** We should talk about that.

**7:** When we’re out.

**CAT:** Agreed.

## 7  Seeing the saucer

**Situation:** They reach a viewpoint over the impound area.

**7:** There. That’s my ship.

**CAT:** The saucer?

**7:** Yes.

**CAT:** Good. I was beginning to worry you meant one of the mining carts.

**7:** We need to get down there and release it.

**CAT:** Then we have a destination.

## 8  Boarding and escaping

**Situation:** They release the saucer and reach its entrance.

**7:** Get aboard.

**CAT:** After you.

**7:** You don’t know how to fly it.

**CAT:** An excellent reason for you to go first.

Once both are safely inside, let the sound and pace settle. Their relief needs space before more exposition.

## 9  The larger interior

**Optional exchange:** Use only if the entrance reveals the saucer’s larger-inside design.

The cat stops and looks around.

**CAT:** This is considerably more ship than I was expecting.

**7:** Is that a complaint?

**CAT:** I’m reserving judgment until I find the kitchen.

## 10  The first meal

**Situation:** They are safe, hungry and beginning to relax. This expands the intended free-range chicken joke; the final two lines are proposed.

7 opens the refrigerator.

**7:** So, what do you eat? Canned food?

**CAT:** Chicken. But only free-range.

She turns to look at him.

**7:** You’re particular for someone I just met in a prison.

**CAT:** Having experienced confinement, I feel strongly about it.

## 11  Choosing to stay

**Situation:** After their immediate needs are met, 7 asks about his plans.

**7:** What happens now? Do you go back?

**CAT:** I was sent to get you out. You’re out.

**7:** I thought you were supposed to help me.

**CAT:** I did.

She pauses.

**7:** With what happened down there, I mean.

**CAT:** I don’t know what happened down there.

**7:** Neither do I.

A quiet beat.

**CAT:** Then perhaps I should stay.

**7:** You don’t have to.

**CAT:** I know.

Let his choice stand without explaining his affection aloud.

## 12  Discussing the vision

**Situation:** The cat helps 7 examine what happened. This conversation leads to the next objective.

**CAT:** Start with what you remember clearly.

**7:** The place. Before it was ruined.

**CAT:** And the people?

**7:** I saw them. I don’t know who they were.

**CAT:** Did anything seem familiar?

7 considers the question.

**7:** I don’t know how to answer that.

**CAT:** You don’t have to decide yet. Tell me what you saw.

The next lines depend on the vision we choose:

**7:** [Describes a specific image, symbol, structure or event.]

**CAT:** [Suggests a possible connection and explains why it might matter.]

**7:** Are you sure?

**CAT:** No. It’s somewhere to start.

If the player receives multiple leads:

**CAT:** We could follow the place you saw, or find someone who might recognize it.

The player chooses. Destination names and clues remain placeholders until the first vision is finalized.
